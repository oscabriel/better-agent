# Attention-Respecting Orchestration

Better Agent will treat the user's Attention — their finite, serial judgement capacity — as the scarce resource the product is architected around, not agent supply. Thinkspace Agents may produce freely, but work that needs human judgement accumulates in a per-user Review Queue under Backpressure, maintained and prioritized by the Coordinator, and never auto-merges to the world. We are choosing this over a spawn-maximizing orchestration dashboard because adding agents optimizes a non-bottleneck: total throughput is capped by the single serial human who must review, reconcile, and approve, so the product's job is to make that judgement go further rather than to make spawning cheaper or the user feel busy.

Considered alternatives: a multi-agent control center that scales by running-agent count and live activity; auto-merging agent output once a Permission exists to reduce friction; per-Thinkspace notifications with no cross-Thinkspace batching. The consequence is that Better Agent paces and batches work to the user's review rate and may feel quieter or less busy than a fleet of parallel agents, in exchange for protecting the user's understanding of their own system and avoiding the orchestration tax of unread merges, shallow reviews, and stale mental models. This reinforces ADR-0003 (drafts/Approvals as the default holdpoint for external mutations) and gives the Coordinator from ADR-0001 an explicit attention-protection responsibility beyond creation and routing.
